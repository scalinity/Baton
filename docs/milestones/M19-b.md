# M19-b — Pause: stand the relay down, or one lane

## 1. Identity

- **ID:** M19-b. **Objective:** a person can stop Baton's work on purpose, all of it or one lane,
  and bring it back, without the stop being read as a crash or undone by the tick.
- **User-visible result:** `baton pause` stops every running lane and admits nothing new until
  `baton resume`, which resumes each paused lane by id and lifts the stand-down. `baton pause
  <milestone>` and `baton resume <milestone>` do the same for one lane. A paused lane is never
  sighted as a crash, never notified as stalled or long-running, never retried from a wait and never
  recovered by the ladder.
- **Size:** Medium, about 3–4 hours; high effort. Second of four: M19-c makes a paused lane keep
  its place under the cap, M19-d shows it in `baton status`. Neither is started here.

## 4. Requirements, scope, non-goals

**Why.** D-101 recorded that Baton has no pause verb and that standing the relay down means
`launchctl` outside Baton. On 2026-09-24 the person, out of usage, stopped two running lanes with the
Stop button; Baton read the stop as a crash (M19 fixes what happened next). A session that runs into
the usage limit on its own already ends with an `api-error` artifact, opens a wait that holds the
model for every project (REQ-STOP-13) and is resumed when the window rolls; what the person had no
way to say was "stop now, on purpose, and leave it stopped until I say".

**The state, from the log.** A lane is *paused* while its current attempt has a `pause` event with
none of these after it, in log order: a `resume` of that attempt with outcome `delivered` or
`forked`; a `pause_ended`; a `dispatch` of the milestone. A consumed `complete` closes the lane
through `lanes_open` as now, and a paused lane that completes is simply closed. Order is the log's
index, never the timestamp, so equal-time events fold correctly; an old attempt's pause never
attaches to a later dispatch. A `hold`, a `crash_sighting`, a notification or a ruling event ends
nothing.

`derive_in_flight` returns a third list, `paused`, and a paused lane appears in neither `in_flight`
nor `no_row`. That one placement is what keeps every watcher off it without editing the watchers:
`crash_check` reads `no_row`; the stall, long-running and question checks, `stops_run`, the
keep-awake derivation and planning read `in_flight`. A paused lane whose session has a live row (the
person reopened it in Claude.app) is listed in `paused` with its pid; `stops_run` writes one
`pause_ended` for it (`reason: live outside Baton`), and from the next tick it is in flight as any
other lane.

**The stand-down.** `baton pause` with no milestone writes a `hold` `{model: "all", cause:
"pause"}` first, so nothing is admitted from that moment, then pauses every open lane that is not
parked, not taken over and not already paused, one at a time, as below. `derive_dispatch_hold`
answers `all` while a `pause` hold stands — today `all` is true only for a `budget` hold or two
limited models, so without this change the hold would bite nothing. Only `baton resume` lifts it:
`derive_holds` closes a hold by its own model and cause, and the budget and reserve lifters write
only their own causes. Run while a stand-down already holds, `baton pause` writes no second hold and
pauses whatever is still running.

**Pausing one lane.** Refused, with nothing written, for an unknown milestone, a milestone with no
open lane, a parked lane (answer it with `baton answer`; a `question` park's session is waiting and
spends nothing), a taken-over lane (D-008: it is the person's), and a lane already paused. Otherwise:
find the session's job in a successfully read listing (`job_of_session` any-state). With a live row:
`claude stop <job>`, then `stop_settle`. The pause is written only if the listing was read and the
session has no live row afterwards; an unreadable listing, a stop that does not settle, or a live row
with no job id refuses the pause, writes no event, and says the session may still be running. With
no live row on a successfully read listing there is nothing to stop and the pause is written at
once: that is the 2026-09-24 shape, a session already stopped from Claude.app, and `baton pause
<milestone>` afterwards is how the person tells Baton it was on purpose. The event is
`pause {session, scope: "lane" | "relay"}`.

**Resuming.** `baton resume <milestone>` refuses a lane that is not paused, and a paused lane with a
live row (the next tick ends that pause). Otherwise it calls `resume_session` with an empty job,
kind `unpause`, class `paused`, which composes the continue template, runs the flagless
`claude --bg --resume <uuid> "<text>"`, and writes `resume` with `resume_kind: unpause`. `delivered`
ends the pause; `forked` ends it on the copy through the existing `copy_fork` handling; `refused`
leaves the lane paused and prints the CLI's reason. A refused unpause is a deliberate act's result,
not a recovery's: it is excluded from the failure-ending definition in both `ladder_position` and
`derive_ladder`, exactly as a refused ruling already is. `baton resume` with no milestone resumes
every paused lane that way, then writes `hold_lifted {model: "all", cause: "pause"}` if a stand-down
holds, and names any lane that stayed paused. While a stand-down holds, `baton resume <milestone>`
resumes that lane only and the hold stands.

**Every route that could start or resume a paused lane.**

- The ladder and the crash sighting: a paused lane is in neither list they read.
- An automatic wait retry: `stops_run` resumes a due wait unless `stops_standing_by` says the lane
  stands by, and today that is only a park or a takeover; it gains "paused". This is the route that
  would otherwise undo a stand-down taken because of a usage limit.
- The tick's dispatch pass and the redispatch rung: a stand-down holds every model through
  `hold_bites`. For a single paused lane, the dispatch pass must not open a new attempt over it:
  prove with a fixture that an open lane's milestone is not a candidate; if it is, stop and report,
  because the fix belongs to M19-c's admission work.
- `baton answer` (a ruling) and `baton allow --resume`: each is a person's explicit act on that
  lane and delivers a `resume`, which ends the pause. That is by design; say so in SPEC.
- Hand `baton dispatch` of a paused milestone opens attempt n+1 over it today, because
  `verb_dispatch` refuses only when a live row carries the name. M19-c refuses it; this milestone
  documents it as the one remaining route.

**What a pause is not.** Not a completion: dependants stay waiting. Not a start: an unpause is a
same-session resume, which REQ-BUDGET-03 does not charge, and a budget hold lifting never touches a
`pause` hold. Not a gap: a paused lane is still open, and the gap rule's meaning, Baton not running
while work was open, is unchanged. A completion artifact that lands while the session settles is
consumed by the normal inbox rules.

**Concurrency.** Both verbs take the lock through `lock_take` in `bin/baton`, as every mutating verb
does, and read the log and the listing only after taking it. A live tick makes the verb wait or
report busy exactly as `baton answer` does; there is no second lock and no unlocked append.

**Non-goals.** No cancel or abandon verb, no project deregistration, no per-project stand-down (the
cap and the stand-down are the Mac's), no inference of intent from a Stop-button press, no change to
the budget pause. The cap is M19-c's and `baton status` listing paused lanes is M19-d's; until then a stand-down
shows in `baton status` through its existing hold line, and `baton pause` and `baton resume` print
every lane they act on.

## 8. Fixtures, verification, acceptance

Fixtures only: the `claude` shim, a fixed clock, a fixture log and listing, and the `sleep` stub so
a settle timeout costs no real time. Run each scenario twice and `sh tests/run.sh`. Assert event
contents, ordering, the shim's recorded argument vectors and unchanged counters.

- **One lane.** A running lane: one `claude stop <job>`, one `pause {scope: lane}`, the lane in
  `paused` and in neither other list. Unknown, not open, parked, taken over, already paused: refused,
  log unchanged.
- **Already stopped.** A lane with no row on a read listing: no stop call, one `pause`, and across
  ten ticks no `crash_sighting`.
- **Settle fails.** Listing unreadable, stop never settles (stub `sleep`, shim row kept live), live
  row with no job id: no `pause` event; the message says the session may still be running.
- **Interleaving.** Equal-time `pause` and `crash_sighting`; a `hold` and its lift; a ruling event;
  an old attempt's pause followed by a new attempt's dispatch; a `consumed complete` after a pause.
  Each folds by index exactly as §4 says.
- **No watcher fires.** Seed a paused lane whose transcript and dispatch are older than
  `stallMinutes` and `longRunningHours` under the fixed clock, beside an unpaused positive control
  of the same age: the control is notified, the paused lane is not, over ten ticks.
- **No wait retry.** A due `rate_limit` wait on a paused lane: no `wait_retry`, no resume; the same
  wait unpaused retries as today.
- **Stand-down.** Two running lanes and a third eligible candidate: one `hold {all, pause}`, two
  `pause {scope: relay}`, and no `dispatch` on the following ticks; a budget hold lifting during it
  lifts nothing of the pause. A second `baton pause` writes no second hold.
- **Resume.** One lane: one flagless `--bg --resume <full uuid> "<text>"` with no other flag, one
  `resume {resume_kind: unpause}`, the lane in flight on the next tick. Refused: the lane stays
  paused and the ladder's failure count is unchanged. Forked: the lane continues on the copy. All:
  every paused lane resumed, then one `hold_lifted {all, pause}`, then admission as before.
- **Live outside Baton.** A paused lane whose row comes back live: one `pause_ended`, then in flight;
  a second tick writes nothing.
- **Person routes.** `baton answer` and `baton allow --resume` on a paused lane end the pause through
  their delivered resume.
