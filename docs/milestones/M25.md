# M25 — A dispatched session cannot kill what it did not start

## 1. Identity

- **ID:** M25. **Objective:** the deny list stops a dispatched session from killing processes by name
  or pattern, so one session's cleanup cannot end another session's run or Baton's own standing check.
- **User-visible result:** a session that types `pkill -f tests/run.sh` or `killall sh` is refused;
  stopping its own background run by the pid it started still works.
- **Size:** Small, about 1–2 hours; medium effort.

## 2. Dependencies and entry conditions

None in the plan. `lib/permissions.sh` is edited by no milestone in flight. Settings files are frozen
into many fixtures; expect their expectations to move with the deny list.

## 3. Required reading

`CLAUDE.md` (the hard rule "Never kill a process you did not start"), `CONTEXT.md`, `CONTRACT.md`;
`docs/SPEC.md` REQ-PERM-01 to REQ-PERM-05; `docs/DECISIONS.md` D-026, D-048, D-112, D-153. Code, at
the merged `main`: `lib/permissions.sh` `permissions_deny_rules` (31–50) and its callers.

## 4. Requirements, scope, non-goals

**The measured finding.** On 2026-10-01 at 22:51:37 (02:51:37Z) M19's session ran
`pkill -f "tests/run.sh" ; sleep 1; pgrep -fl "tests/run.sh"`, to stop a full-suite run of its own it
had started in the background by mistake ("I'm stopping it and combining it with the single-scenario
filter"). The pattern matched every `tests/run.sh` on the Mac, including the standing checks Baton
was running on M15-c's merge (`aed2d5ef`, started 22:30:24) and M15-e's (`ff0d9710`, started
22:40:54). Their output stopped at 22:51:46 and 22:51:43, mid-way through two different scenarios,
with no `FAIL` line; Baton recorded both checks `failed` with exit 143 at 22:52:05 and 22:52:10 and
parked the project `main-broken` twice. REQ-PERM-04 has "two deny classes and nothing else",
privilege escalation and Baton's own state, so nothing refused the command.

**Scope.**

1. **A third deny class: processes by name.** `permissions_deny_rules` adds `Bash(pkill:*)`,
   `Bash(killall:*)` and the `Bash(*…*)` fragments that catch them inside a longer command line, in the
   style D-026 already uses and with its stated limit. `kill <pid>` stays allowed: a session that
   started a process knows its pid.
2. **REQ-PERM-04 says three classes**, with this one's reason, and the decision records why `kill` by
   pid is left open.

**Non-goals.** No process sandboxing, no attempt to make the class complete (D-026), no change to the
other two classes. Making an interrupted check rerun instead of parking is M25-b's.

hard rule in `CLAUDE.md` covers that, and the class says so. Record the decision at the next free
