# M19 — A recovery that reaches a person

## 1. Identity

- **ID:** M19. **Objective:** a confirmed crash whose session has left the `claude agents` listing
  is recovered by session id, and a recovery Baton cannot perform is recorded, counted once, and
  reaches a person instead of repeating in stderr.
- **User-visible result:** a lane whose session was stopped from Claude.app comes back on the next
  tick with a flagless resume by id, or a redispatch when no transcript remains; a lane Baton
  cannot recover is parked `ladder-end` with the reason it could not, within three ticks.
- **Size:** Small to Medium, about 2–3 hours; high effort. First of four: M19-b adds the pause
  verbs, M19-c and M19-d what a paused lane keeps and shows. None is started here.

## 4. Requirements, scope, non-goals

**The measured finding.** On 2026-09-24 the person pressed Stop in Claude.app on two running lanes,
M15-c (session `7f9e3517`) and M15-d (`a716bde4`), a minute after their dispatch. That interrupted
their turns and left both processes live and idle (M19-e reads that Stop as a pause). Eleven hours
later, for a reason not measured, both processes ended and both rows left the listing.
`crash_check` logged `crash_sighting` 1 at 23:07:53 and 2 at 23:08:58 for each; the second
sighting is a failure ending, so `ladder_position` read one failure and named the resume rung.
`ladder_step`'s resume rung then found no job for the session (`job_of_session` returns a job id
only from a row, and there was no row) and returned after printing
`resume rung refused: no job identifies session <id> in the listing` to `~/.baton/launchd.err`. It
wrote no event, so the ladder never moved past its first failure, and the same line was printed on
every tick for a week with no message to the person. A hand `baton dispatch` started attempt 2 of
each on 2026-10-01. Whether Claude.app archived the two sessions was not measured.

Two facts make the fix small. `resume_session` already accepts an empty job: it skips the stop
and runs the flagless `claude --bg --resume <uuid> "<text>"`, and it already writes a `resume`
event whose `refused` outcome `ladder_position` counts. And REQ-STOP-10 contradicts itself: it
calls a row that is *absent* a crash, then refuses recovery when "the listing identifies no job",
which an absent row never can.

**Scope.**

1. **The resume rung, by id.** In `ladder_step`'s resume rung, when the tick's listing (read
   successfully at the top of the tick) holds no row at all carrying the session, there is nothing
   to stop: call `resume_session` with an empty job, kind `continue`, class `process gone`. The
   transcript test keeps its place: no transcript on disk takes the redispatch rung as now. A row
   that carries the session but no job id, with a pid or without, is the one case left to refuse (item 3).
2. **The redispatch, by absence.** In `redispatch`, when no row at all carries the outgoing session,
   skip the stop and settle and go on to `dispatch_try`; a row with a job keeps the existing stop
   and settle; a row with no job id, or a stop that does not settle, refuses (item 3).
   Holds, the budget room and a project park keep deferring exactly as they do, with no event.
3. **A refused rung is an event.** Every refusal of the resume or redispatch rung that happens
   *before* the CLI is asked writes one `rung_refused` event — `rung` (`resume` or `redispatch`),
   `reason` (the sentence now printed), `session` — through `log_event` under the tick's lock. It is
   a failure ending in both `ladder_position` (`lib/stops.sh`) and `derive_ladder`
   (`lib/derive.sh`), which must keep one definition between them. A refusal by the CLI itself is
   not doubled: `resume_session` already writes `resume` with `outcome: refused`, which already
   counts, and no `rung_refused` is written for it. A deferral (hold, budget room, park, unreadable
   listing) is not a refusal and writes nothing.
4. **Counted once, never re-observed.** One recovery action contributes at most one failure ending.
   This holds by construction and is asserted by fixture: a `rung_refused` is the newest failure
   and nothing has acted after it, so the next tick takes the *next* rung, never the same one —
   resume, then redispatch, then escalate. `rung_refused` is a kind of its own and not a `resume`,
   because `crash_check` resets its sightings on any `dispatch` or `resume` of the attempt, and a
   refusal that started nothing must not re-arm the sighting pair of the same dead process.
5. **The escalation carries the reason.** The ladder's end is the existing `ladder-end` class,
   disposition `human-required`. When the newest failure is a `rung_refused`, the escalation's
   `last_detail` is its `reason` and its sentence names "a recovery Baton could not perform".

**Non-goals.** No pause or resume verb (M19-b). No change to how a crash is sighted or confirmed,
to the cap, to the budget, or to any wait. No new liveness probe and no model call.

## 10. Risks and decisions this milestone settles

A by-id resume into a session that came back between the listing and the call is a resume into a
live session, which the CLI answers with a copy; `resume_session`'s fork handling is the guard and
is not weakened. Refusing on a live row without a job id stays, because Baton cannot stop what it
cannot name. Record the decision at the next free D-number read from `main` when it is written.

**Main advances while a session runs.** Other lanes merge into `main` during a dispatch, so the
commit a session started from is rarely the one it merges into. Two lanes that each mark their own
`docs/MILESTONES.md` row and append to the tail of `docs/DECISIONS.md` conflict on both files even
when their code never meets: a merge simulation on 2026-10-01 of M15-c against M15-d, each marking
its row and appending a decision, conflicted on both. So:

- At startup and again before the close-out merge, run `git log --oneline HEAD..main`. If `main`
  moved, merge `main` into this branch in this worktree first, so a conflict is resolved here and
  never in the canonical checkout.
- In `docs/MILESTONES.md`, keep every side's complete row change — status, dependencies, new rows,
  gates — never only the word `done`. In `docs/DECISIONS.md`, keep every row from both sides. Never
  resolve a document by taking one side whole, and never with `-X ours` or `-X theirs`.
- After every reconciliation, clean or not, check that no D-number appears twice; renumber only
  this session's own entries to the next free number on `main` and fix every reference to them. A
  reused number does not always produce a conflict marker.
- This milestone's `done` is written on `main` after the merge and after the standing check passes
  there, as `CONTRACT.md` clause 3 orders it, never on the branch before the merge.
- Merge only into a clean canonical checkout. If another session has uncommitted work there, do not
  commit, stash or clobber it: wait up to ten minutes, then write `stopped` with reason `other`,
  naming the uncommitted paths, and go no further.
- After the merge, run `sh /Users/danny/Documents/Apps/Baton/tests/run.sh` by absolute path, read
  it to completion, and record the `main` commit it ran against; a merge that is textually clean can
  still break a scenario another lane added.
