# M10-b — The tick's own clock, while it is working

## 1. Identity

- **ID:** M10-b. **Objective:** stop a tick reporting itself as an outage, and stop it advising a
  person to remove a lock it is holding.
- **User-visible result:** a completion whose standing check takes eleven minutes produces no
  "Baton was not running" message, and `baton status` during that check says a check is running
  rather than that the lock may be stale.
- **Size:** Small, about 1–2 hours; medium effort.

## 2. Dependencies and entry conditions

M10 complete and installed. M10 made the tick able to spend minutes inside step 2: it runs the
project's standing check itself, on a detached checkout of the commit a completion claims, under
the tick lock (D-148). Nothing else in Baton took that long before, so two rules that measure
against wall clock were never wrong until now.

## 4. Requirements, scope, non-goals

- **Covers:** the two person-facing statements M10 made untrue. Both were verified against the code
  rather than inferred, and the first is deterministic rather than a race.
- **The gap.** `derive_gap` measures `now − last-tick` at step 7. The marker is the *previous*
  tick's, written after that tick released its lock, so it is about one interval old when this tick
  starts. A proved completion runs the standing check inside step 2 — 652 s measured for Baton's own
  suite — so by step 7 the age is past two intervals. `dg_closed` then counts the `consumed`
  event this same tick wrote, whose `at` is after the marker, so `lanes > 0` and `report` is true.
  The first completion Baton proves with its own check therefore sends a Mac message saying the
  relay was not running for the length of the check it was running.
- **The stale-lock line.** `lock_stale_report` tells anyone running a verb during a hold longer than
  its threshold that the lock may be stale and can be removed if no verb is running. A verb *is*
  running, for up to `deadline_seconds`. `lock_stale_break` itself is safe — it checks `kill -0` —
  so the risk is a person acting on the advice, not Baton acting on it.
- **Scope:** measure the gap against the clock the tick started with rather than the wall clock at
  step 7, and say the holder is alive when it is. `tick_run` already captures `tr_now` at its top.
- **Non-goals:** moving the standing check out of the lock (D-148 settled that, and the state
  machine it would need is not this); a notification taxonomy change (M15's); rotating the log.

- **Consumes:** `tr_now`, `derive_last_tick`, `iso_epoch`.

## 7. Implementation checklist (ordered)

1. A scenario that fails first: a completion whose check outlasts two intervals under the frozen
   shim clock, asserting that no `gap` event and no Mac message follow. `completion-timed-out`
   shows how to make a check take a controlled length of time.
2. Give `derive_gap` an optional as-of and measure the marker against it; keep the closed-lane test
   as it is, since a lane closed after the marker is still a lane the gap would have covered.
3. Pass `tr_now` from `tick_run`'s step 7. Leave step 3's `gap_check "$tr_rows"` on the default.
4. Have `lock_stale_report` say the holder is alive when `kill -0` says so, and drop the removal
   advice in that case only.
5. Run §8, update the documents, review, fix and close out.

## 8. Fixtures, verification, acceptance

- **Fixture-run:** a long check with no gap reported; a genuine outage still reported; a stale lock
  with a dead pid still reported as stale; a live lock reported as held. Then `sh tests/run.sh`.
- **Acceptance:** no `gap` event follows a tick whose own work spent the interval; an outage that
  really happened is still reported; the stale-lock line never advises removing a lock whose holder
  is alive.

## 10. Risks and decisions this milestone settles

- **The gap's meaning.** It is "Baton was not running", not "the marker is old". Measuring against
  the tick's own start is what makes those the same sentence again.
- Three counted `lib/` files, which is why this is its own milestone rather than part of M10
  (D-152): M10's budget was two, and the split rule triggers at three.
